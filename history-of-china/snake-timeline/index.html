A NOTE ON HISTORY

Time is precise. History has edges.

This is a curated introduction. Early dates are approximate; Xia belongs to the traditional historical account. Dynasties often overlapped, and their territories changed. The main ribbon is a reading route through history, not a claim to exclusive rule.

Three Kingdoms, Jin, and the Northern and Southern Dynasties have separate bands. Jin began in 266 before Wu fell in 280; Sui began in 581 before it conquered Chen in 589. Side lines preserve these overlaps.

The three broad navigation sections are editorial choices for this atlas. The detailed dynastic groupings are informed by the National Museum of China’s Ancient China exhibition. The chart begins with Xia, so the exhibition’s prehistoric section is outside its scope. Qin unification in 221 BCE marks the conventional beginning of imperial China. The links are starting points for exploration; dynasties and states often overlap. The Republic link covers its mainland period, while its continuation in Taiwan has a separate entry.

Dates before the Common Era use BCE; later dates have no suffix. There is no year zero. The atlas ends in 2026. Each reading card includes source links and analysis. Date notes distinguish traditional accounts, textual formation, and securely dated events.
