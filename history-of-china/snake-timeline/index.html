ABOUT THIS ATLAS

How to use this Chinese history atlas

This site is an interactive timeline of Chinese history from the traditional Xia dynasty to the present. Choose a dynasty, period, person, or event to read a short illustrated explanation with sources.

Many early dates are approximate, and the existence of Xia as a dynasty remains debated. Dynasties and states often overlapped, and their borders changed. The thick ribbon orders the main periods by date. Thin parallel lines show selected states and periods that existed at the same time. For example, Jin began in 266 before Wu fell in 280, and Sui began in 581 before it conquered Chen in 589.

The atlas uses three sections to make the timeline easier to browse: Early China, Imperial China, and Modern China. These are editorial choices. The detailed dynastic groupings draw on the National Museum of China’s Ancient China exhibition. Qin unification in 221 BCE starts Imperial China here. The Republic entry covers mainland China from 1912 to 1949; its continuation in Taiwan has a separate entry.

BCE marks dates before the Common Era. Later dates have no suffix, and the calendar has no year zero. The timeline ends at 2026. Each card includes source links, image credits, and notes where dates or interpretations are disputed.
