In 690, Wu Zetian took the imperial title and founded her own Zhou dynasty, interrupting [[tang|Tang rule]]. A woman occupying the emperor’s position broke established expectations. Keeping that position required a working government, loyal supporters and ways to persuade others that her rule was legitimate.

## Choosing officials

Wu expanded recruitment through examinations, opening opportunities beyond established court connections. Successful candidates could owe their advancement to her support. This did not mean that ability alone determined promotion: she also favoured relatives and sought the prestige of aristocratic family connections. New routes into office worked alongside older forms of privilege.

## Poetry and court connections

Her support for writers made the court an important place to seek recognition. Access was uneven, and writers formed new poetic communities as they pursued opportunities or faced exclusion. Poetry belonged to this world of friendships, patronage and political ambition. Wu’s government depended on the relationships around her as well as on her own unusual position.
