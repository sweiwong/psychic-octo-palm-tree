Li Shimin’s path to the throne passed through an ambush in 626. His followers killed Crown Prince Li Jiancheng and their brother Li Yuanji at the palace’s northern gate. Their father later abdicated, and Li Shimin became Emperor Taizong.

## Securing the throne and its story

The killings left Taizong with a difficult claim to justify. His court brought collaborative historical writing into the palace and gave him a larger role in accounts of the dynasty’s foundation. Control of the government also gave the victor influence over how the struggle would be remembered.

## The celebrated emperor

Taizong later acquired a [[zhenguan-government|reputation for good government]]. That achievement and the violent succession are both parts of his reign. The deaths of his brothers are well established, while accounts presenting his victory as inevitable or entirely justified need more caution. Later readers inherited a story shaped partly by the court that benefited from the coup.
