Li Bai and Du Fu wrote in a world of friendship, travel, court connections and political ambition. Their poetry includes responses to war, displacement and disappointed hopes around the [[an-lushan|An Lushan Rebellion]], alongside humour and intimate exchanges.

## What a poem could do

Poems maintained friendships, marked journeys, sought recognition and commented on government service. Writing belonged to social life as well as personal expression. Both poets worked within this world, even though later readers often contrasted Li Bai’s inspired wandering with Du Fu’s concern for suffering.

## War in a poet’s voice

Du Fu’s war poems convey how catastrophe could be experienced and understood. Their words are shaped by selection, literary references and a speaking voice; they are not population records. Later collections and commentaries helped make particular poems classics. Their lasting power comes partly from how carefully individual experience was turned into language.

## Note

About 740–770 locates the mature poetic world of the two writers; it is not their shared lifespan. Li Bai (李白) died in 762 and Du Fu (杜甫) in 770. Individual poem dates can be disputed.
