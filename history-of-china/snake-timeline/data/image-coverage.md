# Encyclopedia image coverage

Verified locally on 5 September 2026. No images were published or uploaded to an external service.

- **205 of 205 current cards have images**, including all 22 additions in `chart_research.js`.
- **149 distinct image URLs**. The seven existing image selections are retained.
- **149 of 149 current URLs returned HTTP 200 and an image content type** in the final combined delivery check. No known broken current URLs remained.
- Every card has alternative text, a tailored caption, credit, linked source record, reuse label and license link, and dimensions.
- Museum and Wikimedia source records were read for object identification, image authorship, dimensions, and reuse terms. Primary institutional collection records are identified in the credited Commons records where supplied.

## Selection and evidence

Images favor named historical objects, manuscript or printed pages, dated documentary photographs, and identifiable sites. Later portraits are identified as later representations; modern photographs are identified as present-day or dated site views. Political maps are explicitly modern reconstructions with approximate boundaries. No generated likenesses or stock photographs are used.

Shared imagery is deliberate. The same archaeological object can accompany a dynasty and an event connected to it; a contemporary regional map can locate several neighboring states. Reuse does not imply that an object belongs to every state whose card displays it. The captions explain comparisons and the actual object or map date.

Particular corrections made during review: Fu Hao is a reconstructed tomb display; the Tang riding card shows an actual female-rider figurine; Two Tax uses Tang cash; the Tang Code shows a Yuan printed edition of the code; Fei River uses a pre-campaign Former Qin map; Wuyue uses a document issued by Qian Liu; Former Shu uses Wang Jian’s tomb; Hong Kong uses its handover monument, not unrelated flags.

## Practical limits

- Remote hosting can rate-limit later requests. Ten small original-file URLs were replaced with standard 330/500-pixel previews after rate limits; these replacements passed. Larger new assets generally use 960-pixel previews. Existing supplied URLs are preserved.
- Some uniquely relevant archival originals are below 960 pixels, particularly the 1989 Tiananmen photograph, early printed pages, and several historical photographs. They remain at honest source resolution; no invented detail or artificial enhancement was added.
- The 1950 Marriage Law card shows a **later official Marriage Law text**, explicitly labeled as later, because a securely reusable, directly relevant original-1950 image was not established in this pass. It must not be relabeled as the 1950 enactment.
- Some small successor states use a contemporary regional map or a clearly identified neighboring/predecessor object. These are contextual illustrations, not direct photographs of a named ruler or event.
- Public-domain and Creative Commons labels follow the linked file records. Attribution is retained separately from the ancient object’s authorship, including required ColBase, Auckland Museum, Scott Semans, and Karl-Heinz Wellmann credit wording.
- Historical images retain the script visible on the original object or document. Their captions and the application’s Chinese labels remain independent of that authentic historical writing.
