The four existing corrections are:

- Red Cliffs, 208, now belongs to late Eastern Han rather than the Three Kingdoms period beginning in 220.
- Buddhism at about 350 is a broad fourth-century process across divided China. It has no single-government parent.
- Liao retains 907–1125 and its thematic Song grouping, with no containment under a period beginning in 960.
- Warring States retains the draft dates, 481–221 BCE, but is classified as a period with no Eastern Zhou containment parent. Other chronologies use about 470 or 475 BCE for its start. Eastern Zhou ended earlier, in 256 BCE.

Period labels are historical groupings, not governments. A regime's parent period means chronological membership, not political subordination. Northern Wei begins in 386, before the conventional Northern and Southern Dynasties period begins in 420, so it has no parent. Eastern Jin is independent of the Sixteen Kingdoms grouping. The Sixteen Kingdoms selection is illustrative, not all sixteen states.

Five Dynasties spans 907–960. Ten Kingdoms spans 902–979 as the envelope of the selected constituent dates: Wu's 902 date precedes Tang's fall, and Northern Han survives until 979. This envelope is an editorial grouping, distinct from the usual combined period label 907–979. Later Shu, Southern Han, Southern Tang, Wuyue, and Northern Han continue after 960. Chen continues until 589 although Sui starts in 581.

The chronology uses cited museum and scholarly conventions. Date notes disclose alternatives for Western Wei, Later Zhao, Former Qin, Later Tang, Later Jin, Later Han, Wu, Chu, Southern Han, Jingnan, and Southern Tang. These include calendar conversion, princely recognition, imperial proclamation, and surviving remnant distinctions. Year-only boundaries do not imply that governments changed on January 1. Negative years and the existing simple subtraction convention for duration remain unchanged.
